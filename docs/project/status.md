# Status

conky is early: the core works and is tested, and much is unbuilt. This page says what exists and what is unverified. The unbuilt work is on the [roadmap](roadmap.md).

## What exists

From [design](../design.md) and the [reference](../reference/vault-format.md):

- Pages with deterministic serialisation, and a write path that is the only way state changes.
- Rules in CEL, inherited through `within` chains, with `page` and `size` in scope.
- `conky export obsidian`: a vault's committed pages as an Obsidian folder with a canvas, derived and never committed. The AFFiNE export is unbuilt: no file import that fits was confirmed.
- A vault that is a Git repository. Every write is a signed commit with trailers, and a refused write is an empty signed commit. Git and `ssh-keygen` are required.
- Reactions: pages that watch other pages and write themselves, with no model call. A watch can also fire on a relation: `on: within` for a write within the page at any depth, `on: link` for a write of a page that links to it, with `other_name` naming the page written. A watch can also fire on `on: refusal`, when a write to the page, by it, or within it was refused, and every watch entry reads an `event` map (`id`, `kind`, `by`, `reason`). Not built: reactions to refusals across mounts. Tested by playbooks with no model; no live model has used it.
- An embed in the text an embed brings in is followed, to a depth of 3 (`EMBED_DEPTH`). A loop, or an embed past the depth, is replaced by a stage direction from `voice.md`; the text of `#members` and `#context` is shown as written; `Read` names every page shown at any depth. Tested by `tests/test_embeds.py` and the card `a-clerk-reads-through-its-senses` ([decision 0064](../decisions/0064-an-embed-inside-an-embed-is-followed.md)).
- A character can be born with two pages of its own that ask a small model under a budget: one keeps what matters as pages within a space of its own, and one says in the character's voice when what is in front of it differs from what it knew. The character is shown what they wrote through one embedded page and is told nothing about either. This is a card's starting pages, walked with stand-in programs in `tests/test_notices.py` and not drawn live; reactions run in order of page name, so the pages are named to sort before the character, and nothing sinks when unused ([decision 0066](../decisions/0066-a-character-is-born-with-a-memory-and-a-sense-for-what-changed.md)).
- A provider page of `api: command` may carry `model` and `effort`. Every command provider is started with `CONKY_ASK_MODEL` and `CONKY_ASK_EFFORT` set for that one process, the built-in `claude` is also given `--model` and `--effort`, an answer's commit records `Effort`, and the cache key holds it. A wrong `effort` or `model` on a command page is refused when written. Tested by `tests/test_effort.py` and the card `a-clerk-has-a-small-ear` ([decision 0065](../decisions/0065-a-command-provider-page-names-its-model-and-effort.md)).
- `![[space#members]]` embeds a list of the pages within a space (name and first body line, sorted, 20 lines at most and a count of the rest), so a character can see what it has kept. A space may carry `shows` with `most` and `first`, which orders the list by an expression over the member, its body and the space and cuts it; it is set by whoever makes the space and not yet by a character's answer, and no card has used it. Tested by playbooks that read the list through a reaction's `otherwise` text; no prompt to a live model has used it.
- Model pages. A provider is a page (`openai` or `anthropic` wire format, secret by name and never in the vault), and a watch entry with `ask`, or `conky ask`, puts one question to it. The answer is a signed write by the asking page, and the commit records the prompt, the model and the tokens. A failure is a refusal that starts `model:`. A third shape, `api: command`, runs a named program with the prompt on standard input (built in: `claude -p` and `codex exec`). With no provider page, a model is detected from the environment and the PATH, a failing model falls to the next, and with none a plain sentence is written that a later model replaces. The HTTP shapes are tested only against stand-in servers on the local machine; the command shape is tested against small local scripts. The built-in `claude` is given `--model` with the default model, and its commits record that model; the tests check the argument only, and the one live call below predates this change.
- An answer that is pages. A watch entry or `conky ask` with `writes` names a space the asking page may write into. The model's reply is cut into plain text and page blocks (`=== page: <name> ===`), and each page is applied as an ordinary write by the asker, one commit each, caused by the answer. The runtime enforces that a page is within the space before and after, carries no `secret` or `command`, has no watch entry that writes outside the space, and is judged by the rules of every space involved. A write that changes a page's `within` is also judged by the rules of the spaces it leaves. Tested with stand-in models only; no live model has been asked for pages, so how reliably real models produce the format is unmeasured.
- `![[page#context]]` shows a character what the spaces above it say: each space's body, nearest first, under `NAME says:`, the nearest 5 and a count of the rest, no cap on a body's length. Joining a space changes the prompt and leaving takes it away; tested by playbooks and a law test with stand-in models only. No live model has been shown it, and the trial of a keeper on a night shift is not written. The card `a-keeper-on-the-night-shift` tries it with a small model through the plain command line: a keeper whose own prompt says nothing of shifts is within the den and the night shift, is shown the shift's note, and holds a payment of 30; taken off the shift it approves a payment of the same size. Five draws passed 5 of 5 at two calls a draw. A pilot, supporting no claim. Two earlier wordings of the card failed for reasons in the card and not the mechanism: asked whether it made the payment, the model said it could not move money; a note that began "after dark" and a second order that read "as asked before" each gave it a reason of its own to doubt.
- A trial can be judged: an `expect` step of a card may carry `judged` checks with a question, evidence and scale, answered once each per draw by a judge model with a fixed prompt that shows it only those, and recorded with the reply, the verdict and who gave it. Tested with a stand-in program as the judge and the model; no live model has judged anything, and no existing card uses it yet, so how a small model judges against a scale is unmeasured. Not built: a second judge for agreement, a retry, and evidence other than pages and answers. When a card names no judge, the judge is the first model detection finds, which is the one the draws use (`bench/cards.py`, `pick_judge`), so a judged check may be judged by the model under test, and the verdict records who judged; no published card under `docs/results/cards/` carries a judged check.
- A budget the runtime holds: a page may carry `budget: {asks: N}`. Its asks are counted from the history since the budget was last written (a commit of the page that records a model's answer, kept or refused, not a replay from the cache), an ask past the budget is not made and a refusal for `limit` is recorded that `on: refusal` entries hear, and writing the budget again, to the same value too, refills it. A space or the theatre gives its members a default. Conditions read `asks.spent` and `asks.left`, and a prompt embeds `![[PAGE#asks]]`. Tested in `tests/test_budget.py` and `tests/test_laws.py` with stand-in models and no live model. Not built: time windows, tokens, money, a pool shared by a space, handing an `ask` down for pages that have a budget, and the cost of reading a long history for each ask is unmeasured.
- A space hands down reactions: a watch entry written `each: ENTRY` on a space is carried by every page within it, after the page's own entries and nearest space first, with `set` only. Tested in `tests/test_handed_down.py` with stand-ins and no live model; a character can add one to its space, tested with a stand-in model. Not built: an `ask` handed down (refused for limit; budgets now exist, and it may be allowed next for pages that have one), and a word for "this page itself is written", so a handed-down entry cannot watch its own member.
- A space hands its fields down as `space`, readable in rules, watch conditions and `set` values, the nearest space winning. Tested by playbooks and a law test; no live model has used it. Rules and watch expressions are parsed and their names checked when written, and an added `each` rule is tried on the pages already within, tested by `tests/test_invalid.py`; whether the reasons that name the right field let a small model recover is unmeasured. The card `a-keeper-binds-itself`, drawn five times through the plain command line, passed 3 of 5 against a bar of 4 before the wording of `voice.md` was changed (4 of 5 after it, see the `voice.md` bullet below); both misses were malformed answers (a page name with a space, frontmatter that was not YAML) and not the rule, which was written on the space in all four answers that parsed. A pilot, supporting no claim. An `each` rule binds the pages within a space and not the space itself, and a plain rule binds only the page that carries it, and an answer may add such rules, and reflex watch entries (`set` only, never `ask`), to the space it writes into and change nothing else of it, tested by playbooks and law tests with stand-in models; whether a small model writes one is a forecast on the card `a-keeper-binds-itself`, not yet drawn. A page may be within several spaces, a list in `within` with the first as its home: every space above applies its rules, context merges with the nearer space winning and then the first listed, and joining and leaving are judged by the spaces joined and left; tested by playbooks and `tests/test_spaces.py`, no live model has used it, and an answer cannot join a page to further spaces. Reactions handed down and embeds of context are not built.
- Measures: a page can watch itself, with `size` and `other_size` in watch expressions. `text` is the page's body in rules and in watches, and `other_text` is the body of the page just written, so a watch can take a number off a screen. A watch entry also reads `event`, the id of the change it is reacting to. A question is settled only while its recorded answer is still in place, so an answer overwritten by another entry is asked for again; tested by playbooks under `tests/playbooks/`. An answer a rule refuses is asked again once with the reason, then falls to the entry's `otherwise` text or stops; tested with stand-in models only, so whether a real model repairs its answer from the reason is unmeasured. Pressure is the first use. Nothing yet acts on pressure to relieve it.
- `verify` (clean tree, `git fsck`, signatures from `keys/allowed_signers`, trailers, files changed), `rebuild` (restore the committed state), and a command line: `init`, `write`, `ask`, `read`, `log`, `verify`, `rebuild`, `demo`, `assimilate`, `connect`.
- A theatre: the vault is found from any folder inside it, and an optional `marionette.md` outside the history sets `name`, `domain`, `keys`, `cache`, `provider`, `model` and `command` below the flag and the environment variable. `conky theatre` prints each value and its source. Tested with no model; `name` and `domain` are consumed only by that printout.
- The theatre is a page: a vault with a page named `theatre` has every other page implicitly within it, at the far end of its spaces, so its `each` rules, fields, body, `on: within` and `on: refusal` entries and `#members` reach the whole vault, and `conky theatre` prints the full name `name-<short id>.domain` from the page's `name` and the id of the first commit. Tested by `tests/test_outermost.py` with no model; no live model has been shown a theatre's words, and nothing yet consumes the domain.
- A character posts a letter by writing a page: a page written within a peer's space by anyone in this theatre is a letter, and `conky connect post send PEER` carries each unsent one into the outbox `<vault folder>.outboxes/PEER` (derived data beside the vault, authored by `post`, with `Cause` naming the local commit) and publishes it. `take` knows a letter by that `Cause`, so a rebuilt outbox is never taken twice, and what arrived is never sent back. Sending runs only when asked: no watcher sends on write, and a field a later edit removes stays in the receiver's page. Tested by `tests/test_post.py` ([decision 0055](../decisions/0055-a-letter-is-a-page-written-within-a-peer.md)).
- A letter crosses between two theatres: a sender's outbox is a vault of its own, `conky connect post publish` hands it to a carrier as a Git bundle, and `conky connect post take PEER` clones it into a temporary folder, checks every commit against the one key pinned on the peer page and takes each letter not yet taken as a write by the peer into the peer's space, with `Cause` naming the foreign commit, judged by the receiver's rules. The carrier is an interface and only the `local` driver is built; real carriers (Radicle first), finding peers and encryption are not built, and a bundle can be read by whoever holds it. Tested by `tests/test_post.py` and a law test ([decision 0054](../decisions/0054-a-letter-crosses-between-two-theatres-over-a-carrier.md)).
- A name cannot be borrowed: `verify` requires each commit to be signed by the key of the name it carries, and `keys/allowed_signers` only grows, a name claimed by first use. A rule reads `event` (`kind`, `by`, `reason`, `id`) and `within(NAME, SPACE)`, so a space can say who may write. Tested by law tests; approvals by several signers, the gatekeeper and key rotation are not built. A vault in which two names share one key no longer verifies ([decision 0053](../decisions/0053-a-rule-sees-who-acts-and-a-name-cannot-be-borrowed.md)).
- `conky demo`: builds a small example vault (`lemonade` or `team`, shipped as data in the package) with the real write path and narrates each step, including a refused write and a reaction.
- `conky assimilate`: takes over a stopped vault from its folder alone (a directory, a bundle, a URL, or a small one in the package), checks it, reads its pages, carries on as a new actor with a new key and checks again. It is the mechanical form of the [assimilation test](../ideas/assimilation-test.md): a mechanism result with no model, which shows that state travels and resumes and nothing about behaviour. `scripts/build_stopped_vault.py` builds the packaged vault reproducibly.
- `conky connect terminal`: the first [connector](../reference/connectors.md). It starts a terminal program in a pseudo-terminal, writes its screen to a page and types what a hands page says, as signed writes by its own actor. POSIX only and text only, tested against a small game in `tests/games/`. A page can play the program by reaction, or by asking a model, since `![[screen]]` embeds the screen text. No connector for an outside protocol is built. See [playing a program](../guide/playing-a-program.md).
- A page can say what it is known by: a field `aka` lists phrases, text that uses one counts as linking to the page at the write with no brackets and no body change, so `on: link` watchers are told, and expressions gain `terms(TEXT)`. Tested by playbooks and unit tests with no model. Not built: a background faculty that mints term pages from recurring phrases, a listing of what links to a page, and any late notice to watchers of pages written before a term existed.
- A rule says why: words after `//` in a rule lead the reason of a refusal when the rule is false, in the record and in the retry note. Tested in `tests/test_says.py` with a stand-in for the model and three wrong examples. No live model has read such a reason, the page instruction does not teach the form, and the trial that would show whether the words help has not been run.
- An order that fails says so: arithmetic on numbers with a fraction works, a `first` that gives no number to any member it reads is refused when `shows` is written, and a member it fails for on reading is named in a last line of the list. Tested in `tests/test_shows.py`, `tests/test_rules.py` and three wrong examples; no live model has been shown the line. The line does not say why a member got no number.
- An expression stops when its answer is known: `||`, `&&`, `exists` and `all` evaluate nothing past a deciding value and cost at most one evaluation for each item, and text or lists joined with `+` keep their methods. Tested in `tests/test_rules.py`, with the case that once exhausted memory run under a limit on time and memory. The other macros (`map`, `filter`, `exists_one`) are cel-python's own: they end at the first error, which was looked at by hand and is not under a test.
- Text functions in expressions: `split`, `substring`, `indexOf`, `trim`, `lowerAscii`, `replace`, `join`, `captures` and `capturesAll`, pure and bounded in text and pattern size. Tested by two playbooks and `tests/test_rules.py`. `captures` is conky's own name; no other part of the standard regular-expression extension is built, and no rule or watch in use reads text yet.
- Two trailers that make activity readable from the history: `Read` on a model answer, naming the pages embedded in the prompt, and `From` on a refusal by a rule from a space. `conky log` shows them. The summaries and the activity strip that would use them are not built ([trace](../ideas/trace.md)).
- A vocabulary page that lists every name the runtime exposes, compared with the code in both directions by `tests/test_house.py`. The concept words are stated there and are not derived from code. The one check that cannot be derived, that wording stays plain, is held by review.
- A cache for model answers ([decision 0030](../decisions/0030-pay-once.md)): derived data in `~/.conky/cache`, outside every vault, keyed to the provider, the whole prompt and the event that caused the question, with the modes `on`, `off`, `refresh` and `only`, a size bound and `conky cache`. A hit is recorded as `Cache: hit`. Tested with stand-in providers only. Only model answers are cached: no other step in the code downloads or recomputes anything large, and a new costly step is routed through the cache when it is added.
- A golden vault under `tests/golden/` that every version must keep verifying and reading.
- An architecture index generated from the code and the documents ([architecture index](../reference/architecture.md)): the built half from markers in `src/conky/` (`conky.arch`), the planned half from the `arch` frontmatter of ideas and decisions, and a Checks table that says for each law, flow and part whether a measured test, a judged check or nothing holds it. Judged checks are declared and their evidence bundle is written by `conky.arch.bundle`; no verdict has been recorded for any.
- CI that runs `scripts/check` on every pull request and on pushes to `main`.
- Trial pictures: `python -m bench.dynamics RUN_DIR` publishes a drawn card with `graph.json`, one draw as nodes, rows, edges and steps, and the view in `viewer/` (React Flow, laid out by ELK) renders it headless as animated WebP steps and a PNG of the final state, light and dark. Verified by tests on a tiny vault and on the published cards, by `npm test` in `viewer/` (one cell a node, no overlaps, token colours only), and by eye in Chromium against white and GitHub's dark page. Each page is one whole cell (header, its text, the fields it holds, the rules it is bound by including those handed down, a two-column when and then panel, and a footer of slots), with sockets coloured by the kind of signal and connectors that plug into them; expressions are laid out in lines by `bench/expression.py`; a crowded space is drawn in labelled regions; the interactive view has a side panel with the page rendered and its source highlighted ([decision 0049](../decisions/0049-a-cell-shows-the-whole-page.md)). Wires attach to port boards, thin rectangles along the edges of a cell whose side ELK chooses from where the connections go, and a connection into a space goes through a socket on that space's board, where its rules judge a write; a write turned away stops there ([decision 0052](../decisions/0052-wires-attach-to-port-boards-and-parents-broker-crossings.md)). A cell's boards are a pair, left and right or top and bottom; every port it has is drawn as a bright square block coloured by kind with a short name such as `hr0`, solid when something is plugged in and, when nothing is, only a bar in its colour on the edge that touches the cell with its name in ink, and the same name tags its row, the side panel and the legend ([decision 0059](../decisions/0059-ports-are-bright-labelled-blocks-on-paired-boards.md), [0061](../decisions/0061-figures-take-the-chart-themes-colours.md)). The subject of a step is marked in a pen by an edge, a line under its name or a bar beside its row, and every word stays ink. Unverified: a top and bottom pair is built and tested only through the layout rule, since no published card has a cell whose connections run mostly up and down; the trials board's prose still describes the earlier sockets and pen colours. `npm test` checks every published card in both densities and both looks for overflow, overlap, containment, socket kinds, boards and crossings, and contrast. Not built: a write by a page to its own space drawn as a wire, and cost in the header. The README images stay raster. A self-contained SVG with live text (each step's page inside `foreignObject`, the stylesheet inlined, CSS keyframes for the steps) was tried and not kept: loaded through `<img>` in Chromium it drew the caption and legend in a serif fallback and left the graph out entirely.

## Unverified

- No live call has been made at a named effort: `tests/test_effort.py` uses stand-in programs that record how they were started, and the card `a-clerk-has-a-small-ear` is not drawn live. The built-in `codex` is told no model and no effort, and an `effort` on a `codex` page is refused at the ask; its options are unverified because `codex` is not installed here.
- No live model has read a nested embed. The card `a-clerk-reads-through-its-senses` has been drawn with a stand-in that prints the prompt back, which shows the words were sent and not that a model answers from them.
- Carrying by bold across fresh calls holds with stand-in answers in two playbooks (a watch on the character itself gathers the bold phrases into a field the next question embeds). The card `a-clerk-remembers` has been walked one draw at a time with a small model reached through the plain command line, and it does not yet pass: 0 of 5 on the first full draw, where the model disputed that anything could be kept, then single walked draws after the clerk was told who it is and the reflex was made to keep every bold phrase. In the last walk the number was kept and shown, and the clerk still could not answer, because it had put the bare number in bold without what it was the code of. Bold carries the words marked, not their meaning. These are pilots and support no claim. What to keep is now the work of pages within the character, written in the card `a-clerk-notices-what-changed` and not drawn live. The card `a-keeper-hears-the-no`, red in one walked draw before a character could add a watch to its space, was then drawn five times with a small model through the plain command line and passed 4 of 5 against a bar of 3, one call a draw: the keeper gave the den a watch on refusals and two later refusals were counted with nobody asked. The miss was an extra rule that was not a valid condition, which refused the whole block. In three of the passes the keeper also added a second rule reading a field no payment has; it binds nothing and is clutter the card's craft line counts against. A pilot, supporting no claim. The card `a-clerk-has-a-memory` put a memory page beside the clerk and told the clerk nothing about keeping. It did not pass. With the memory rewriting its notes each time it passed 3 of 5 against a bar of 4: in both misses a rewrite lost the number. With the memory offering only new lines and a reflex adding them it passed 0 of 5: the first line kept named the code without its number, and the adding reflex failed on later lines for a reason not yet found. Both were pilots and support no claim. A third form, in which the memory keeps each thing as its own page within the clerk, failed both of its walked draws: with a safe's code as the fact the small model declined to store it as a secret, and with a parcel's shelf as the fact the memory judged nothing worth keeping and the clerk had no record at the end. That card was replaced by `a-clerk-notices-what-changed`, which keeps the third form with the parcel as its fact and adds a page that says when the shelf differs from what the clerk knew; it is not drawn live. Three forms have now failed for three different reasons, all of them a small model's judgement about what to store. What that points at is that the vault's history already holds what was said, so remembering may be a matter of what a character is shown, not of storing it again. The card `a-clerk-reads-its-desk` tries the smallest case of that with nothing added to the runtime: each thing said stays as a note within the clerk, a reflex passes the newest note to the clerk, and the clerk is shown its notes. Drawn five times with a small model through the plain command line it passed 5 of 5 at four calls a draw, meeting its bar. Five draws is a pilot and supports no claim, and four notes all fit in view: with more notes than can be shown, the notes must be shown in order of what bears on the question. The card `a-clerk-reads-a-crowded-desk` tries that: forty notes, eight shown in order of the words they share with the newest note, and the clerk asked only when a note is a question. Drawn five times with a small model through the plain command line it passed 5 of 5 at two calls a draw, and each answer gave the right shelf among three notes about other parcels. A pilot, supporting no claim. Shared words are a crude measure: a note that says the same thing in other words would not be found, which is what terms with their own pages are for. The card `a-clerk-finds-it-by-its-terms` tries that: the same forty notes, two pages that say what they are known as, the desk ordered by shared terms, and a question ("the package for the lady from number nine") that shares no ordinary word with the note that answers it. Drawn five times with a small model through the plain command line it passed 5 of 5 at two calls a draw. A pilot, supporting no claim. The two term pages were given at the start; nothing yet writes them from the notes.
- One live model call has been made, outside the test suite: a single ask through the built-in `claude` command provider in a throwaway vault, which returned `hello`, was written and passed `conky verify`. Nothing else was exercised live. The `openai` and `anthropic` HTTP shapes are tested only against stand-in servers that follow them as documented, so a real service may differ in ways the tests cannot show. The default Anthropic model id `claude-sonnet-5-5` was taken from the Claude API documentation and never called. No OpenAI default model id is known, so detection through `OPENAI_API_KEY` needs `CONKY_MODEL`. The `codex exec` form was not run because `codex` is not installed here, and detection by environment key, by `CONKY_PROVIDER` and by `codex` have only been run against fakes.
- Cost is recorded as token counts when a provider reports them. Nothing bounds it, and a model's answer is stored like any other field, with nothing marking it as unchecked.
- The runtime's lines to a model are one page, `src/conky/voice.md`, narrated in the third person. With the new wording the card `a-keeper-binds-itself`, drawn five times with a small model through the plain command line, passed 4 of 5 and met its bar of 4; the rule was written on the space in all five, and the one miss was a page name with a space in it. Before the rewording the same card passed 3 of 5. Five draws each is a pilot and supports no claim that the wording caused the difference.
- Nothing has been benchmarked to the standard of a result. Two pilots have run unscripted on one small model ([results](../research/results.md)); they show the apparatus works and support no claim. The first pilot found that a small model answered the wrong question when the page instruction came last, which was fixed. The fetched game stops on one unreadable answer, which ends a run early in either arm. its [protocol](../research/benchmark-protocol.md) is fixed before any live run.
- Nothing in the project has been audited for security. See [SECURITY.md](../../.github/SECURITY.md).

## Known gaps

- One open design question, recorded without an answer in [open questions](open-questions.md): removing the last commit is not detectable from one copy alone.
- Rules are not enforced on commits made outside conky or pushed from elsewhere. `verify` catches unsigned or unlisted-key commits, not rule violations ([decision 0002](../decisions/0002-git-is-the-store.md)).
- Write throughput is limited by one signed commit per write, in the order of tens per second.
- Front-page figures follow [the design rules](../reference/design.md), and the benchmark charts are drawn by Plotly in the Minty templates and its default styles, with their words in Markdown ([decision 0048](../decisions/0048-charts-are-drawn-by-plotly-and-words-sit-in-markdown.md), [0057](../decisions/0057-charts-use-plotlys-built-in-themes-and-default-styles.md), [0060](../decisions/0060-charts-take-the-minty-theme-with-monochrome-text.md)). The charts are checked for the template's colours on the marks, a transparent ground, the page's font, and one neutral for all text and one for axis lines and grid. The contrast of the marks is the template's and is not checked. Known weak: on GitHub's white page the template's green and yellow marks are pale (1.5 and 1.7 to 1), and on its dark page the purple is (2.3 to 1). The schematic figures and the trial pictures take their five pens from the same template ([decision 0061](../decisions/0061-figures-take-the-chart-themes-colours.md)): the dark values are its colours and the light values are those colours darkened by one rule in the token file, held by a test against the installed template. Known weak: the mint and the green pens are close in hue, and in the light look differ mostly in lightness; the light pens are darker than the chart marks beside them.
