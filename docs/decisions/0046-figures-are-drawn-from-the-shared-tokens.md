# 0046 Figures are drawn from the shared tokens

## Context

The front page carried figures in three looks. The schematics were one ink with one yellow accent. The benchmark plates had their own literal colours, two soft neon pens, a rule under each title and flame graphs in nine pastel tints, with a paper colour per look. The demo recording sat in a dark terminal window with rounded corners and traffic-light buttons in both themes. The project title and nothing else were drawn as an image. Readers found the plates hard to read, the colours unexplained and the sheets too empty.

`src/conky/tokens.json` already held one set of values for the trial animations: a ground (paper, ink, mute, sunken, raised, hair), five pens with meanings, pen weights, dashes and sizes.

## Decision

Every figure a person sees on the front page is drawn from `src/conky/tokens.json`, and nothing that draws for the front page holds a colour of its own.

- The ground is monochrome. Text and data are ink; mute is for secondary text; hair is for gridlines and leaders only.
- Five pens, each used only where it means something: pink for a refusal and for the subject of a chart, amber for something a character added and for a broken rule, blue for what is heard, teal for what was kept or passed, violet for a model's thought.
- Backgrounds are transparent. Each figure has a light and a dark file, switched by `<picture>` and `prefers-color-scheme`. The dark ink is a neutral near-white, `#f2f2ef`, and mute `#a3a7ad`, because the figures sit on GitHub's own dark page, where a neutral white reads best.
- Text in a front-page figure uses the faces GitHub renders the page in (`type.embedded_text`, and `type.embedded_code` for code and tabular numbers), named once on each figure.
- Charts are drawn by Plotly with its own themes, not from the tokens ([0057](0057-charts-use-plotlys-built-in-themes-and-default-styles.md)); the ink ramp and chart colours are gone.
- Text is text: the README title is a heading, not an image. Images are for figures and marks.
- Brand marks are not restyled and keep their own colours: the sponsor wordmark and the three-band ornament are exempt from the colour audit, by name.

The rule in `CLAUDE.md` that said "one ink, one yellow accent" now names the tokens.

Prior art: the maintainers' design system, from which the tokens, the pen meanings and the principles (a printed scientific magazine by day, a console by night; colour only where it carries meaning; no gradients, shadows or rounded corners) are adapted. GitHub's contribution heatmap for the ramp. WCAG 2.1 for the contrast thresholds the audit computes.

## Consequences

- `bench/kit.py` reads the tokens for the plates; `tests/test_bench_kit.py` fails when the plate code holds a colour literal, when a published plate carries a colour that is not a token, names a face more than once, uses opacity, or puts text below 4.5 to 1 or a data stroke below 3 to 1 against GitHub's page colours.
- The schematics are generated by the maintainers' tooling from the same file, and its own check fails on a colour or a face that is not a token.
- The colour emoji on the comparison figure are gone: they were colour without meaning.
- What was guaranteed before and is kept: every figure has a light and a dark file, the plates are regenerated from the run files, and the same data gives the same bytes. Given up: the yellow accent and the dark terminal window in the light theme.
- Deferred: the docs site, the viewer's interface, embedded fonts, the remaining results-page charts, and text of at least about 11 pixels on screen in every schematic figure (the schematics are laid out at 880 units and shown at 800, so their smallest labels land near 8 pixels).
