# Changelog

All notable changes are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/). Versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). The vault format is held stable by the golden vault test in `tests/golden/`.

## [Unreleased]

- The benchmark charts are drawn by Plotly from one template on the shared tokens: black and white with outlined flat bars, one spot colour for the subject from a chart palette that is a carve-out from the five pens, and no words in the image beyond labels and a legend. The README's benchmark block gains a heading and a caption for each chart, the scores as a Markdown table and one footer line. Plotly and kaleido are the optional `bench` dependencies ([decision 0048](docs/decisions/0048-charts-are-drawn-by-plotly-and-words-sit-in-markdown.md)).
- The front page's figures and charts are drawn from the shared tokens: a monochrome ground, five pens used only where they carry meaning, transparent light and dark files, the faces GitHub renders the page in, and flat charts in a four-step ink ramp. The README title is a heading, not an image. The rules are in `docs/reference/design.md`.

- New card `a-clerk-finds-it-by-its-terms`: the crowded desk with a question put in other words, found through two pages that say what they are known as.
- New card `a-clerk-reads-a-crowded-desk`: forty notes on the desk and eight shown, in order of the words they share with what was just said; the clerk is asked only when a note is a question.
- New card `a-clerk-reads-its-desk`: nothing is stored twice. Each thing said stays as a note within the clerk and the clerk is shown its notes, so a fact given once is given again three notes later with no memory kept.
- New card `a-clerk-has-a-memory`: a memory page beside a clerk that is told nothing about remembering. It does not pass yet; the draws are recorded in the status page.
- The auto-linking idea now says when linking happens: known terms are recognised as part of the write, and the background pass only mints new terms and catches up older pages.
- Fixed: a model reached through a program on the machine could read files. The built-in `claude` command now runs with its tools, tool servers and commands off and its own instructions replaced by one plain line, and every command provider starts in an empty folder. Live runs made before this, including the three trail pilots and the first walks of the cards, reached their model the old way and are marked as such.

### Changed

- A plain rule applies to the page that carries it and to no other; only an `each` rule applies to the pages within a space. The examples, the stopped vault, the guide and the tests that relied on a plain rule being handed down now use `each`, and the fixed statement says a rule can read `space.FIELD` as well as `page.FIELD` ([decision 0036](docs/decisions/0036-a-plain-rule-stays-with-its-page.md)).

### Added

- A character is shown what its spaces say: `![[page#context]]` embeds the bodies of the spaces above a page, nearest first, each introduced by `NAME says:` and separated by a blank line, at most 5 spaces and then a count of the rest. Joining a space changes what a character is told and leaving it takes that away, with no model deciding and nothing copied. A page may no longer carry a field named `context` ([decision 0047](docs/decisions/0047-a-character-is-shown-what-its-spaces-say.md)).
- A page may be within several spaces: `within` is a name or a list of names, the first being its home. Every space above applies its rules, context merges with the nearer space winning and then the first listed, joining and leaving are judged by the spaces joined and left, and `on: within` and `on: refusal` tell every space. An answer cannot join a page to further spaces ([decision 0045](docs/decisions/0045-a-page-may-be-within-several-spaces.md)).
- A page can say what it is known by: a field `known_as` lists phrases, and text that uses one counts as linking to the page when it is written, with no brackets typed and no body changed, so `on: link` watchers are told. Expressions gain `terms(TEXT)`, the names of the pages a text uses, for rules, watches and `shows.first`. Minting term pages in the background is not built ([decision 0044](docs/decisions/0044-a-page-is-known-by-its-phrases.md)).
- A space can say what it shows: a field `shows` with `most` and `first` orders and cuts the list `![[space#members]]` gives, by an expression over `page`, `text` and `space`, so a character with more on its desk than fits is shown what bears on the matter in hand with no model call and nothing stored. A space with no `shows` lists as before ([decision 0043](docs/decisions/0042-a-space-shows-what-bears-first.md)).
- A trial is drawn as it happened: `python -m bench.dynamics RUN_DIR` publishes a card's run under `docs/results/cards/<card>/` with `graph.json`, one draw as a graph with steps read from its vault history, and the view in `viewer/` (React Flow, laid out by ELK, in the colours and symbols of the shared token file `src/conky/tokens.json`) draws it, in a browser or headless as animated steps and a picture of the final state, light and dark. D2 is gone. A board of every trial, red ones included, is `docs/research/trials.md`, and the README carries a generated Dynamics block ([decision 0043](docs/decisions/0043-a-trial-is-drawn-as-it-happened.md)).
- An answer that is pages may add reflex watch entries, as well as rules, to the space it writes into: a block naming that space holds `rules`, `watch` or both and nothing else, entries are added and never replace, and an added entry with `ask` is refused with reason `limit` until asks are metered. A character can now make its own space count what it turns away ([decision 0041](docs/decisions/0041-a-character-gives-its-space-reflexes.md)).
- One idea placed in the design as an idea, nothing built: attention (every page hears what it chose to hear, at the cheapest layer that can deal with it), with its layers, encapsulation rules, trial ladder and falsifying test.
- Three ideas placed in the design as ideas, nothing built: standing (what is shown first is decided by a measure), being told (a refusal that a watch can notice) and faculties (a sentence in, the form written by a watching page), with entries 26 to 28 in the seams page and falsifying tests added to the pages they touch.

- New card `a-keeper-hears-the-no`: a character is asked to make its own space count what it turns away. It did not pass at first: a character could not add a watch to the space it writes into, and one walked draw wrote the watch as a rule on a new page. Decision 0041 lets it; the card is not yet drawn since.
- New rule: speech comes from pages and the runtime narrates. Every line the runtime says to a model is in `src/conky/voice.md`, told in the third person as a stage direction, held by the house style and by a test that code and page agree, and nothing a model is shown says whether a page was written by a person or a model ([decision 0040](docs/decisions/0040-the-runtime-narrates-and-pages-speak.md)).
- The card `a-clerk-remembers` tells the clerk who it is, and its reflex keeps every phrase put in bold and no longer only the last answer's, so a fact need not be put in bold again to survive.
- A trial states itself before it runs: every card carries `objective`, `hypothesis` (which replaces `claim`), `success` as data (`draws`, `pass`, optional `calls_at_most` and `sent_at_most`), `forecast`, `par` with a new `craft` line, `dial` and `if_not`. The runner refuses a card missing any, and after the draws prints and records `success met` or `success not met` with the numbers against each bar ([cards](docs/reference/playbooks.md#cards)).
- A rule is proven when it is written. Every rule and watch expression is parsed at the write, a name outside the ones its place has is refused for shape with the names allowed, and an added `each` rule is tried on the pages already within that have the fields it reads off `page`, so a rule about payments is not judged against a page that is not one. A refusal for a missing field says which field was read on which thing and, when another thing has it, to read it there. Every wrong form under `tests/invalid/` is refused ([decision 0038](docs/decisions/0038-a-rule-is-proven-when-written.md)).
- `capturesAll` in expressions, the first group of every match, and a trial of memory across fresh calls: a clerk's reflex gathers what it put in bold into the field it reads next time. Two stand-in playbooks hold the mechanism; the card `a-clerk-remembers` is written and not drawn.
- A refusal can be noticed: `on: refusal` tells the page refused, the page that tried and the spaces it lies within, and every watch entry now reads an `event` map with `id`, `kind`, `by` and `reason`. The old scalar `event` is now `event.id` ([decision 0039](docs/decisions/0039-an-event-can-be-noticed.md)).
- A watch entry can fire on a relation: `on: within` for a write within the watching page at any depth, `on: link` for a write of a page that links to it. The entry reads `other_name`, the name of the page written. A page named `within` or `link` is refused ([decision 0037](docs/decisions/0037-a-watch-can-fire-on-a-relation.md)).

- Cards draw in parallel with `--jobs N` under one shared call cap that refuses an impossible run before it starts, write `observations.jsonl` beside `rows.jsonl` (new pages, rules as written, refusals with reasons, the start of each live answer), and `python -m bench.cards --read DIR` summarises a run ([cards](docs/reference/playbooks.md#cards)).
- An answer that is pages may add rules to the space it writes into, and can do nothing else to that page: a block naming that space is allowed when it holds `rules` and nothing else, its entries are added to the space's rules, and a duplicate is ignored. The fixed statement tells a character so and shows it. Cards may carry a `par:` of three lines, copied into the first row ([decision 0035](docs/decisions/0035-a-character-binds-its-own-space.md)).
- A rule entry may be a mapping with the one key `each`, a condition that holds for every page within the page that carries it, at any depth, and not for that page itself. A character can bind a whole kind of act in one line, and the fixed statement of an ask that writes pages tells it so. Playbooks gain the checks `each_rules` and `within_fields` and the `each_ruled` write; the card `a-keeper-binds-itself` asks for a floor on every payment ([rules and spaces](docs/reference/rules-and-reactions.md#rules-and-spaces), [decision 0034](docs/decisions/0034-a-rule-for-the-pages-within.md)).
- Cards: a playbook whose `says: live` steps a real model answers, drawn N times with `python -m bench.cards`, one row a draw kept in `rows.jsonl`. Playbooks gain the checks `rules` and `answer`, and `says: live` is an error in an ordinary playbook test. The first card is `a-keeper-binds-itself` ([cards](docs/reference/playbooks.md#cards)).
- The order of reactions is stated: watchers of a written page are visited in order of name, each one's own reactions finishing before the next watcher, and a playbook holds it ([reactions](docs/reference/rules-and-reactions.md#reactions)).
- An answer that a rule refuses is asked again once, with one fixed sentence naming the reason after the prompt. The second ask is caused by the refusal. When it is refused by a rule too, the entry's `otherwise` text is written, and without one the asking stops. A refusal for shape, limit or cycle is not retried ([asking a model](docs/reference/rules-and-reactions.md#when-an-answer-is-refused)).
- `conky export` shows a vault as an Obsidian folder (notes, `conky.canvas`, a new `spaces.canvas` overview of nested spaces with page counts, and `who.md`, a table of who signed each page generated from the history) or as Markdown and a zip for Notion's import, under `dist/`, derived and never committed. `export affine` is removed: no AFFiNE file import was confirmed.
- `docs/project/seams.md`: every capability worth pre-empting, the place it will land, what holds that place, and what in today's code it would strain, with seven new idea pages and their roadmap steps.
- The benchmark splits a run's time into the seconds spent waiting on the model and the harness's own, and the score counts the harness's own. The order in which reactions run is stated in the reference and held by a playbook.
- The scores table on the README is one line a harness, best first: a linked name, the version each harness reports of itself, the model, the share that reached the goal, cost, time and a score out of 100 whose weights are stated in the protocol. Harnesses carry one-word names.
- The benchmark reports dollars: `bench/prices.json` holds list prices for a million tokens, the cost plate draws what each run cost against progress with the price in its legend, and the scores table gains a cost for a run. Where a provider reports no token counts they are estimated from characters, and the plate says so.
- The README's benchmark block gains a plate of what each harness had become at the end of a run, flame graphs of time and text, and a compact table of scores.
- `bench.run --cache-mode branch --replay-from RESULTS_DIR [--live-from CALL]` starts a run from an earlier run's record: answers come from it while requests match and from the backend after the first that does not, or from call number CALL. `run.json` gains `branched_from`, `live_from`, `replayed_calls` and `live_calls`; `bench.analyse` and `bench.publish` leave branched runs out and say how many ([protocol](docs/research/benchmark-protocol.md#branching-a-run)).
- `text` and `other_text` in expressions: the body of the page being written (in rules) or watched (in watches), beside `size` and `other_size`. A watch can read a number off a page's body with the text functions ([variables in scope](docs/reference/rules-and-reactions.md#variables-in-scope)).
- `bench/shape.py`: the shape of each benchmark run at its start and its end (a vault's pages and how they watch, ask and sit within each other, read from its Git history; a conversation; a policy), declared by each harness's `adapter.json` and written by `charts.export` as `shapes.json` ([protocol](docs/research/benchmark-protocol.md#the-shape-of-a-run)).
- `bench/record.py`: every benchmark run reduces to one standard row (scenario, harness, model, seed, commit, prompt hash, budget, ending, progress, calls, chars, tokens, violations, kept, tape) and one series, and `comparable` says why a set of arms cannot be compared (different models, seeds, prompts, budgets, commits, scenario versions or backends). What is specific to a scenario now lives in its `scenario.json` and score module, and what is specific to a harness in its `adapter.json`. `runs.csv` and `runs.json` are the standard rows, `series.json` is written beside them, the published page gains a "Compared like for like" section, the README block names the model and the commit, and `bench.publish --kind result` refuses a set that is not comparable ([protocol](docs/research/benchmark-protocol.md#the-standard-run-record)).
- The instruction for an ask that may write pages now comes before the prompt, so the author's question is the last thing the model reads. A pilot run showed a small model answering the wrong question when the instruction came last.
- Contributor and security notes moved to `.github/`, leaving five files at the top of the repository.
- Rule and decision 0033: every roadmap step states the difference it makes against other harnesses and how it is shown.
- The benchmark plate (`bench/plate.py`) is a printout drawn from the standard record alone: what was held equal (model, seeds, budget, commit), every run as a bar from start to goal, the text each harness sent, and the same seed side by side.
- `bench/publish.py` turns a results directory into the charts, the data behind them (`runs.csv`, `runs.json`), the results page and the Benchmark block of the README, with nothing edited by hand. `bench/charts.py` gains the `trail` chart.
- `scripts/check` runs the tests in parallel, and `scripts/check quick` runs only the tests that the changed code can affect.
- Text functions in expressions: `split`, `substring`, `indexOf`, `trim`, `lowerAscii`, `replace` and `join` after the CEL strings extension, and `captures`, which returns the groups of a regular expression match as a list. Text and patterns are bounded at 65536 and 256 characters ([rules and reactions](docs/reference/rules-and-reactions.md#text-functions)).
- `space` in expressions: the fields of the spaces a page is within, the outermost first and the nearest overriding, readable in rules (including those handed down), watch conditions and `set` values. A page within no space reads an empty map, and a missing field is a failed evaluation to guard with `has`. Changing a space does not re-judge its members.
- The instruction appended to an ask that may write pages is now a short description of what the page can do: its name, its space, how a page is written (built from the constants the parser uses), that a page can be a note or carry a rule, and that it cannot write elsewhere. It says the space's members are shown to the page only when the prompt embeds `![[space#members]]`. The Conky benchmark arm's pages prompt embeds its space that way ([scaffold](bench/adapters/conky/scaffold.md)).
- `![[space#members]]` embeds the pages within a space: one line each, `name: first line of body`, sorted by name, at most 20 lines and then a count of the rest. A missing or empty space embeds nothing, and a page may not carry a field named `members`.
- `conky connect terminal --first-line` types only the first non-empty line of the hands page's keys and leaves the rest in the page. It is off by default, and the Conky benchmark arm uses it to keep the reply contract.
- The architecture index, `docs/reference/architecture.md`, generated by `scripts/gen_architecture.py` from markers in the code (`conky.arch`: laws, parts, archetypes, slots, flow steps, measured and judged checks) and from the `arch` frontmatter of ideas and decisions. `scripts/check_docs.py` fails when the committed page differs, and `scripts/check_docs.py` prints a reminder when the paper index was last scanned more than seven days ago.
- A watch entry can read `event`, the id of the change it is reacting to, so a case can mark what it has met without a turn field.
- Dotted names for everything in the architecture index: laws, parts, layers, lanes, kinds of page, slots and their members, kinds of data, places, stages, flows and judged checks are `kind.subject.aspect`, a step is `flow.write.accept#3`, and every entry has an anchor equal to its name. The generator fails when a backticked name under `docs/` names no entry. Wire names are unchanged.
- A benchmark harness under `bench/`, outside the package: the 1975 Oregon Trail (fetched at run time, pinned and hash-checked, run deterministically by a launcher), a metering door for every model call with budgets, byte accounting and a record of calls, a runner with paired seeds, prompt variants, stop conditions, stepping and resume, the `conky`, `loop` and two no-model arms, probes, an analysis with paired bootstrap intervals and result classes, a stage-by-stage walkthrough, and the [benchmark protocol](docs/research/benchmark-protocol.md). No live model has been called through it.
- Pay once: a cache for model answers, kept in `~/.conky/cache` outside every vault, keyed to the provider, the whole prompt and the event that caused the question. The same question for the same event is never paid for twice, and the same words after a new event are asked afresh. A hit is recorded with the trailer `Cache: hit` and no token counts. `CONKY_CACHE` and `--cache-mode` take `on`, `off`, `refresh` or `only`, `CONKY_CACHE_DIR` and `--cache-dir` move it, `CONKY_CACHE_MAX` bounds it, and `conky cache` shows or clears it ([decision 0030](docs/decisions/0030-pay-once.md)).
- `![[page]]` with no field embeds the body of a page. A missing page or an empty body embeds nothing, as a missing field does, and a page read this way is listed in the `Read` trailer like one read by field. A page that asks a model can now read a connector's screen text.
- A vault as a Git repository: every write is a signed commit, and a refused write is an empty signed commit.
- Rules in CEL, inherited through spaces.
- Reactions: pages that watch pages and write themselves.
- Measures: pages that watch themselves, with pressure as the first use.
- `verify` and `rebuild`.
- A command line: `init`, `write`, `read`, `log`, `verify`, `rebuild`.
- A documentation system with frontmatter, a guide, a reference, decisions, ideas and essays, and checks for it.
- Continuous integration on GitHub Actions: `scripts/check` runs on every pull request and on pushes to `main`.
- A vocabulary page, `docs/reference/vocabulary.md`, listing the concept words and every name the runtime exposes, and a check that it agrees with the code in both directions. It replaces the lists of retired and internal words, which are removed.
- Explanatory figures in a pen-plotter style, shown in the README and the concepts guide.
- Prose linting with Vale: a house style in `.vale/styles/conky/` whose errors fail `scripts/check`, and `vale .` for every level.
- Pages that ask a model. A provider is a page (`openai` or `anthropic` wire format, secret by name and never in the vault). A watch entry can carry `ask` in place of `set`, and the answer is a signed write that records the prompt, the model and the tokens. Tested against stand-in servers only.
- A third provider shape, `api: command`: the prompt goes to a named program's standard input and the answer is its standard output. The program comes from outside the vault (`CONKY_COMMAND_<NAME>`, `<name>.command` in the key directory, or the built-in `claude` and `codex`), never from a page.
- A model with no provider page: when an ask names none, Conky detects one from `CONKY_PROVIDER`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `OPENAI_API_KEY` with `CONKY_MODEL`, then `claude` and `codex` on the PATH. A failing model falls to the next, and with none a plain sentence is written, or the watch entry's `otherwise`, and the question stays unsettled so a later ask with a model replaces it. `conky model` shows the order.
- `conky ask`: a one-off question whose answer is written to a page by an actor.
- An answer that is pages: an `ask` entry, or `conky ask --writes`, names a space, and the model's reply may hold pages (`=== page: <name> ===` then the page), each written as an ordinary signed write by the asker, caused by the answer. A page must be within the space, carry no `secret` or `command`, and may not name a wider space in its own watch entries. Refusals start `limit`. Tested with stand-in models only.
- A write that changes a page's `within` is judged by the rules of the spaces it leaves as well as those it joins ([decision 0022](docs/decisions/0022-leaving-a-space-is-a-write-the-space-can-refuse.md)).
- Trailers `Model`, `Model-Name`, `Tokens-In` and `Tokens-Out`, and `model <provider>` marks in `conky log`.
- `conky demo`: builds a small example vault, with `lemonade` and `team` examples, written as notes people leave for each other, and tells its story step by step.
- Styled terminal output in the Charm manner, with rounded boxes and badges, switched by `--color auto|always|never` and `NO_COLOR`; plain output is unchanged.
- `conky connect terminal`: a connector that gives a vault eyes and hands on a terminal program. It writes the program's screen to a page and types what a hands page says, as ordinary signed writes by its own actor. POSIX only. Tested against a small game in `tests/games/trail.py`.
- Trailers `Read` on a model answer, naming the pages embedded in the prompt, and `From` on a refusal by a rule that came from a space, with `read` and `from` marks in `conky log`.
- `conky assimilate`: takes over a stopped vault from its folder alone, checks it, reads it and carries on as a new actor with a new key. With no source it uses a small stopped vault that ships in the package, built by `scripts/build_stopped_vault.py`. It is the mechanical form of the assimilation test and involves no model.

### Changed

- The lens idea page is now the codec page, `docs/ideas/codec.md`: a codec turns one tree into another and comes one per form. `kind.lens` is `kind.codec`, `flow.lens.revise` is `flow.codec.revise`, and `kind.interpreter` is removed, since the go-between is the codec at a connector. Nothing in the code changed.
- A question is settled only while its answer is still in place: the mark `<into>_asked` now digests the prompt together with the answer, so an answer overwritten by another entry is asked for again.
